Bats and surveys before a loft conversion in Worthing

Key facts

  • Bats and their roosts are legally protected, including when no bats are present.
  • Check the loft for droppings or signs before work starts.
  • A licensed ecologist advises if there is evidence or a high chance of bats.
  • Survey order: head height and structure, then design, then any ecology work.

Bats are the surprise that stops builds, so look early.

Why do bats affect a loft conversion?

Bats and their roosts are protected in the UK, and a roost stays protected even when the bats are away. Disturbing or damaging one can be an offence, so work must pause if there is evidence of bats.

What signs should I look for?

Droppings on the loft floor, insulation or window ledges, staining around gaps in the roof or eaves, and any sight of bats entering or leaving. Older tiled roofs with gaps are typical places.

What happens if there are signs?

Stop and take advice from a licensed ecologist, who may carry out a survey. Some bat surveys can only be done in the warmer months, so a late start can push a project back.

What other surveys come first?

A building survey to confirm head height at the ridge, roof structure, staircase position and obvious constraints such as water tanks or chimney breasts. Around 2.2–2.3m from floor to ridge is a commonly used working benchmark for a standing-height room.

Who arranges the surveys?

The survey and design stage is part of any loft conversion. Ask whoever is quoting. For the process, see the timeline.
