Loft conversion rules in Worthing: planning, building regulations and party walls

Key facts

  • Three separate systems apply: planning (or permitted development), Building Regulations and the Party Wall Act.
  • Adur & Worthing Councils handles planning and Building Control for most of Worthing.
  • The South Downs National Park Authority is the planning authority inside the Park.
  • Permitted development caps: 40 cubic metres (terraced) or 50 cubic metres (other houses) of added roof volume.

Before any beam goes in, a loft conversion has to clear rules that are easy to mix up. This handbook takes them one at a time, in the order you will meet them, with the Worthing specifics where they exist.

What are the three sets of rules?

Planning decides whether the work is allowed to happen at all. Building Regulations decide how it must be built. The Party Wall etc. Act 1996 decides what you must do about a wall you share with a neighbour. Each has its own application, sign-off and fees, and passing one does not pass the others.

Where do I start?

Start with permitted development to see whether your plan needs a planning application. Then check conservation areas, read planning in Adur and Worthing, and move on to building regulations and party walls. The timeline shows how they overlap.

Who decides?

Adur & Worthing Councils runs planning and Building Control for the area. Properties inside the South Downs National Park, near Worthing's northern edge, fall under the South Downs National Park Authority for planning instead. Always confirm which applies to your exact address.

Where are the costs?

Council fees are on the Worthing loft conversion costs site. Installer-side guides are at Worthing Loft Conversions.

What is not covered?

Listed building consent, detailed engineering design and legal advice. If your home is listed or you are unsure, ask the council.
