Conservation areas and Article 4 Directions for Worthing lofts

Key facts

  • Parts of Tarring, Central Worthing and the Marine Parade seafront sit inside conservation areas or Article 4 Directions.
  • The historic village cores of Goring-by-Sea, Broadwater and West Tarring are conservation areas.
  • Boundaries are exact; check your address, not just your neighbourhood.
  • Conservation-area homes generally need a planning application for roof extensions.

A road name is a prompt to check, not proof of status. Boundaries follow individual properties, and the only reliable source is the council.

Which parts of Worthing are affected?

Pockets of Victorian and Edwardian streets in Tarring and Central Worthing, the Marine Parade seafront, and the historic village cores of Goring-by-Sea, Broadwater and West Tarring are the areas named in local guidance. Coverage varies by exact address.

What is an Article 4 Direction?

It is a local order that removes specific permitted development rights in a defined area, whatever the general national rules say. It can apply even where a home does not look historic, so check the address.

What does this mean for a loft conversion?

Controls are tighter, especially for anything visible from the street or seafront. A roof extension in a conservation area generally needs a full planning application rather than permitted development.

What about listed buildings?

A listed building has its own consent requirements on top of any conservation-area rules. Ask the council before any design work.

How do I check my address?

Ask Adur & Worthing Councils' planning service. For the cost implication, see planning in Adur and Worthing.
