Building regulations for a Worthing loft conversion: fire, structure, insulation and stairs

Key facts

  • Building Regulations approval applies to almost every loft conversion, separately from planning.
  • Four main areas: structure, fire safety, insulation and stairs.
  • Adur & Worthing Building Control: £1,258.61 inc. VAT (Full Plans) or £1,510.33 inc. VAT (Building Notice) up to 100m², from 1 April 2026.
  • A completion certificate marks sign-off.

If planning asks whether you may build, Building Regulations ask whether it is safe. They apply even when planning does not.

What structure rules apply?

A loft's existing ceiling joists are usually built to hold a ceiling, not a bedroom. Regulations require new floor joists, often with steel beams, sized for the load and a clear path for that load to the foundations. Calculations are normally prepared by a structural engineer and checked at inspection.

What fire safety is needed?

A protected escape route to a final exit, fire doors on rooms off the stairwell and interlinked smoke alarms on every floor the new stairs pass. An escape window in the new room is also commonly required. A two-room conversion often needs a protected stairwell with fire doors on each floor.

What about insulation?

The new roof, any new external walls (such as a dormer's cheeks) and the loft floor must meet current thermal performance standards. Ventilation, particularly for an en-suite, is a related separate requirement.

What are the stair rules?

Part K sets rise, going, headroom and handrail requirements. Loft staircases may have a steeper maximum pitch than a main staircase. The practical side is in the cost site's staircase guide.

How does Building Control work?

You apply either by Building Notice for straightforward work or by Full Plans, checked before work starts, the more usual route for a loft. The council inspects at set stages, for example before structure is covered and at completion, then issues a completion certificate. Charges are in the key facts.
