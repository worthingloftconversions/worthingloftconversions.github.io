Party wall rules for loft conversions in Worthing

Key facts

  • The Party Wall etc. Act 1996 applies when work cuts into, raises or affects a wall you share.
  • Common on Worthing terraces and many semis.
  • Adjoining owners must be notified before work starts.
  • It is separate from planning and Building Regulations.

You can have planning, Building Control and a ready builder, and still be stopped by a neighbour if the wall paperwork is wrong.

Does the Act apply to my loft?

If the conversion cuts into, raises or otherwise affects a wall shared with a neighbour, yes. That is common on terraces and many semis in Tarring, Broadwater and Central Worthing. Detached houses are less likely to be affected.

What do I have to do?

Serve formal notice on adjoining owners before work starts on the shared structure. They are entitled to a response period. If they agree in writing, work can proceed; if not, surveyors are appointed.

What if my neighbour objects?

A dispute leads to one or two party wall surveyors setting out the terms. If either side appoints a surveyor, the homeowner doing the work usually meets the cost. Straightforward notices are often agreed without one.

How does it affect the timeline?

It can delay the start. Serve notices in good time, before the roof is opened.
